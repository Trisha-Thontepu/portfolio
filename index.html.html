Logan Connect

A directory and text-to-book system for Latinx-owned businesses in Logan Heights, San Diego.

It started with my mother's nail salon, Logan Nails Spa on National Ave, and grew into a neighborhood directory with bilingual booking by text message.

The problem

Small, family-run businesses in Logan Heights are often reached by phone or by walking in. Many customers speak Spanish first, and most booking tools assume an English-speaking customer with a new app to install.

The design question was simple: what is the lowest-friction way to find a local shop and book with it, in the language the customer already uses?

Key decisions

  • Text messages, not another app

    Everyone already has SMS. Booking by text means no download, no account, and nothing new to learn.

  • The language follows the customer

    The engine reads the first message and replies in English or Spanish, then stays in that language for the whole conversation.

  • One real anchor, clearly labeled demos

    Logan Nails Spa is a real business. The other five listings are placeholders styled after the neighborhood, and the interface marks them as demo listings so nothing is presented as real that is not.

  • The demo runs the real booking path

    In the full app, the "Try it" widget calls the same engine that a carrier webhook would call. That keeps the demo honest and makes going live a configuration change.

    What comes next

    Building the prototype showed me where it stops being enough. These are the next iterations, in the order I would tackle them.

    1. Owner tools. There is no login yet, so owners cannot manage appointments or edit or add their own listing. That is the biggest gap between a demo and a product.
    2. Real listings. Replace the placeholder businesses with real Logan Heights shops, added by their owners.
    3. A sturdier date parser. The day and time parsing is regex-based. A production version needs a date library and timezone handling.
    4. A live phone number. Point a Twilio number at the existing webhook to move from simulated to real SMS.

    PriceIQ

    A pricing assistant that shows sales reps whether a quote hits its margin target, and closes the gap in one tap.

    I built PriceIQ and supported its pilot during my internship at Bain, for a technology reseller. Reps used to gather deal history by hand before setting a price. PriceIQ matches each new deal to similar past deals, using clustering on closed-won transactions, and turns that history into a recommended price.

    The problem

    Deals were priced from memory and manual research. Each rep pulled their own history, so two reps could quote the same kind of deal at very different margins, and nobody saw a shortfall until the quote was already out.

    The design question: what is the fewest taps between "I have a quote" and "I know it meets target"?
